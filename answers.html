Chapter 2: Chemistry Analytical Methods and Instrumentation

Question 1
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: One control exceeds two SD above the mean and the other exceeds two SD below, so the range between them is more than four SD within one run, which is the R-4s pattern and points to random error. The 2-2s rule requires two controls beyond the same side of the mean, which is not the case when the results fall on opposite sides. The 4-1s rule needs four consecutive observations beyond one SD on one side. The 10-x rule needs ten consecutive controls on one side, which cannot be assessed from a single run.
Question 2
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: The diluted result must be multiplied by the dilution factor to give the concentration in the original specimen, so 14.4 times 2 equals 28.8 mmol/L. Reporting the diluted value would grossly underestimate a clinically important hyperglycemia. Reporting the upper limit would hide a validated extended value that the laboratory can produce by dilution. Dividing by the factor moves in the wrong direction and gives a value that is lower than the diluted reading.
Question 3
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: A large prolactin-secreting tumour can saturate both antibodies separately in a one-step sandwich assay, so the signal falls and the result is falsely low; dilution restores the sandwich and reveals the true concentration. A wide linear range does not protect against the hook effect, which happens beyond the range. Heterophile blocking addresses a falsely high signal, which is the opposite problem. Recalibration at low concentrations would not correct an error occurring at an extreme high concentration.
Question 4
Correct answer: C.
Why the other choices are wrong: A slope of 1.00 with a nonzero intercept means the new method reads about 0.45 mmol/L higher at every concentration, a constant systematic error that a correlation coefficient cannot reveal. High correlation measures how closely the points follow a line, not whether the line is the line of identity. A slope equal to one shows no proportional error, so describing a proportional disagreement is incorrect. Precision is measured by replicate variation and is unrelated to the position of the regression intercept.
Question 5
Correct answer: D.

Question 7

Question 8

Question 9
